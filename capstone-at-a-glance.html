Capstone & In-Person Days at a Glance

A short summary of how our interns build their capstone and what happens on the days they come back to class. The full student-facing pages are linked at the bottom if you want more detail.

Michael Theriault · michael.theriault@bps101.net  |  Austun Savitski · austun.savitski@bps101.net

1How the semester flows

BootcampAbout 3 weeks in class: resume, interviews, employability skills, placement.
At the placementStudents are on site most days and log hours + a weekly reflection.
In-person classEvery other Tuesday, 90 minutes. 7 sessions in the fall.
Capstone BreakfastOne morning in December (Dec 15 this year) for families, mentors and district leaders.

The capstone is 25% of the course grade. The in-person days are where it gets built, a little at a time, so nobody is cramming in December.

2The Capstone: three parts

Each part is scored on the same scale (4 · 3.5 · 3 · 2 · 1). We score each criterion, then average. The format is flexible; the criteria are not.

Part 1 · On stage

Field Experience Spotlight

A prepared 1:30 talk about themselves and their placement. No notes on a phone, no Q&A.

  • ~15 sec: who and where
  • ~30 sec: what they actually did
  • ~30 sec: what they learned / how they grew
  • ~15 sec: what's next
Scored on: content & specificity, growth & insight, delivery, timing, future direction
Part 2 · In the lobby

Field Experience Showcase

Each student staffs a table. Guests walk up and get a 60–90 sec walkthrough, then ask questions. The table has:

  • A Skills Evidence Board (all 10 employability skills, one photo/artifact + one line each)
  • Something real from the job guests can touch
  • A photo board or looping slideshow
  • Their resume and a printed table card
Scored on: pop-up presentation, handling questions, station setup, professionalism, audience awareness
Part 3 · Behind it all

Portfolio

No required checklist, because a welding intern and an accounting intern shouldn't hand in the same thing. Instead it has to answer five questions:

  • What did you actually do?
  • Can you show more than one kind of thing?
  • Can you prove you grew?
  • Would a stranger understand it?
  • Are you allowed to show it?
Scored on: evidence of real work, range, curation, ownership, professionalism (permission & confidentiality)
Where the portfolio lives: each student keeps a "Field Experience" folder in their own school Google Drive and saves one thing from work every Friday. Their weekly reflections become the raw material for the Spotlight talk.

3What happens on in-person days

Same three parts every session, so students know the routine and there's very little re-explaining. 90-minute block.

TimePartWhat happens
0–25Reflection "speed dating"Two facing rows. Teacher calls a question, pairs talk ~3 min, one row shifts a seat. New question, new partner. Questions come straight from the weekly reflection form (what did you work on, a challenge you faced, which skill you used and how).
25–60Capstone: teach + buildA 5-minute mini-lesson on that week's capstone focus (see below), then about 30 minutes of work time on the portfolio, Spotlight talk or Showcase table.
60–85Employability skills packetStudents work two skill worksheets on their own or with a partner. The packet is self-contained, so the teacher is free to circulate and hold 1:1 check-ins about placements.
85–90WrapAttendance, follow-ups from the 1:1s, what's due before the next in-person day.
#Capstone mini-lesson focus
1What the capstone is; start your Drive folder
2Spotlight structure; first draft on the Prep Sheet
3What counts as an artifact; pull and label 2–3
4Showcase expectations; sketch the table
5Midpoint check: range, not just volume; peer read
6Every student says the Spotlight out loud, timed
7Logistics + full dress rehearsal and table dry run
#Skills packet pairing
1Teamwork & Conflict Resolution · Communication
2Problem Solving · Decision Making
3Critical Thinking · Adaptability
4Initiative · Reliability
5Cultural Competence · Planning & Organizing
6Review of all 10 using the district skill indicators
7Catch-up; extra capstone time
What we've learned so far: hear every Spotlight talk out loud at least a week before the event. That one timed run-through catches the two most common problems: talking about the company instead of yourself, and not having a real ending.
