Teamwork & Conflict Resolution
Collaborate effectively, navigate disagreement, and build trust with the people you work alongside.
Skill 1 of 10
Session 1
Name
Date
Real Workplace Scenario

You're two weeks into your placement. You're working on a project with two coworkers. One person keeps making decisions without asking the rest of the team. The other has barely contributed anything. Your supervisor expects the finished project by Friday. It's Wednesday.

What do you do?
The Idea
Teamwork is contributing your part, supporting others' contributions, and adapting to fit the people you're working with. Conflict resolution means addressing disagreement directly and professionally — staying focused on the problem, not the person.
Activity 1 — The Group Chat Criticism

A
Respond in the group chat right away and defend your work
B
Wait until you've cooled down, then message them privately asking for specific feedback
C
Say nothing and redo the section on your own
Why?

Activity 2 — The Disagreement

You and a teammate have fundamentally different ideas about how to approach a task your supervisor assigned. Your supervisor says you need to figure it out between yourselves. In 3–5 sentences, describe exactly what you would say to your teammate to move toward a solution without damaging the working relationship.

Strong responses stay focused on the task (not the person), acknowledge the other viewpoint, and propose a concrete next step.

Where Are You Right Now? — circle one
1
Developing
Tends to avoid conflict or handles it emotionally. Struggles to contribute consistently in group settings.
2
Approaching
Participates in team work but may disengage when conflict arises. Inconsistent contribution.
3
Meeting Expectations
Consistently contributes to team goals. Addresses conflict directly and professionally — focused on the problem, not the person.
4
Exceeding Expectations
Proactively notices team dynamics and adjusts. Mediates conflict between others when needed.

Communication
Speak clearly, listen actively, and write with professionalism across every situation at work.
Skill 2 of 10
Session 1
Name
Date
Real Workplace Scenario

Your supervisor sends you a task with instructions over email. You start working on it, but halfway through you realize you may have misunderstood a key part. You've already spent two hours on it. You're worried about looking incompetent if you ask for clarification now.

What do you do?
The Idea
Communication is the clear, effective exchange of information, adapted to your audience. It also includes knowing when to speak up — staying silent when you're confused or have made a mistake is a communication failure, not professionalism.
Activity 1 — The Clarification Email
The Email — read this first
To: supervisor@company.com · Subject: (none)

hey so i was gonna do the report thing u told me but i got confused on part 2 lol. when can we meet? also idk if i can come in thursday just a heads up

A
Send the email shown above as-is — it gets the point across
B
Don't ask — try to figure it out yourself so you don't look unprepared
C
Rewrite the email professionally: clear subject, greeting, specific question, your name
Why?

Problem Solving
Identify the real issue, analyze the causes, and take action — even when the path forward isn't obvious.
Skill 3 of 10

Name

Real Workplace Scenario

You're covering the front desk alone. A customer walks in clearly upset — their appointment was cancelled but nobody told them. You didn't cancel it. You don't know who did. Your supervisor is in a meeting with the door closed. The customer is staring at you, waiting.

What do you do?
The Idea — 5-step framework
1. Name it — What is the actual problem?   2. Analyze it — What caused it?   3. Options — What could I do?   4. Act — Pick the best option and try it.   5. Evaluate — Did it work? What would I do differently?
Activity 1 — The Jammed Printer

A
Skip the meeting — you can't attend without the document
B
Keep trying to unjam it until the meeting starts
C
Look for an alternative (email the doc, try another printer, ask a coworker) and inform your supervisor
Why?

Real Workplace Scenario

It's Tuesday at 10:00 AM. Your supervisor stops by before heading into a meeting and drops three tasks on your desk:

Task AUpdate the inventory spreadsheet with last week's numbers and email it to the warehouse team. Due today by 3:00 PM.
Task BGo through the general inquiry inbox and respond to any emails that came in this week. No hard deadline — "sometime this week."
Task CPrint and organize handouts for tomorrow's 9:00 AM all-staff meeting. Needed ready first thing in the morning.
You have five hours before you leave today. Where do you start — and how do you think through it?
The Idea — three questions that cut through the noise
1. What has a hard deadline today?   2. What is someone else waiting on?   3. What can flex without consequence? Do #1 first — always.

Activity 2 — Your First Monday Plan

Good planning isn't reactive — it's something you do before the day hits you. Draft a rough daily plan for your first Monday at your placement site.

Prompts to guide you
• Arrival: what time do you show up, and what do you do in the first 10 minutes?
• Morning block: one specific thing you want to accomplish before noon
• Check-in: when do you connect with your supervisor to confirm priorities?
• End of day: what do you wrap up, confirm, or hand off before you leave?
• Tracking: how will you remember what happened and what's next?
Where Are You Right Now? — circle one
1
Developing
Tasks completed in whatever order they come, without considering deadlines. Frequently misses deadlines.
2
Approaching
Aware of deadlines and sometimes prioritizes accordingly, but inconsistently. Reactive, not proactive.
3
Meeting
Consistently prioritizes deadline-driven tasks first. Communicates when workload is unclear. Uses a system.
4
Exceeding
Plans ahead before being prompted. Flags potential conflicts early. Tracks progress others can depend on.
Reflection

Describe a time — at school, in an activity, or in a job — when you had more to do than time allowed. What did you do first, and why? Would you prioritize differently now?
