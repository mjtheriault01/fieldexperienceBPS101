Batavia High School Course Syllabus

Field Experience — Work-Based Learning
Two tracks, one course. Students in the Placement track meet daily for one semester and complete their hours at an outside business or organization. Students in the Enterprise track meet on A days for the full year and complete their hours running their own business or working for a school enterprise (Bulldog Media). Both tracks have the same requirements, the same grading categories, and the same weights — only the source of the workplace evidence differs.

Course Description
A work-based learning course where students complete a minimum of 60 supervised hours of real professional work. Placement-track students earn those hours at a local business or organization aligned to one of seven career clusters. Enterprise-track students earn them operating their own business or working for a school enterprise. All students first complete a hands-on bootcamp covering professional identity, interview preparation, and workplace readiness. The course emphasizes the Illinois Essential Employability Skills, real-world application of classroom learning, and career exploration through direct workplace experience.
Required Materials
Laptop/Chromebook access for weekly logs & reflections, professional attire appropriate to your work (varies by site), and a fully signed Training Agreement on file before Day 1. Placement track: reliable transportation to and from your placement site. Enterprise track: a signed remote work agreement and a named client or advisor who will complete feedback forms.
Prerequisite
Junior or Senior standing, teacher/counselor approval, and a completed application and interview process.

Grading / Assessments
Types of assessments: Bootcamp deliverables (resume, self-assessments, mock interview or client pitch, checklists), weekly hours logs, weekly reflections, employer or advisor evaluation, employability skills self-assessment, capstone presentation.

Your grade is built from two halves. Professional Obligations asks a single question: did you do the required thing, on time? Professional Growth asks a harder one: how well did you perform? In this course reliability is not a bonus — it is half the grade, because it is the first thing every employer measures.

Professional Obligations — did you do it, on time?50%
Scored Meets / Does Not Meet. Submitted on time = full credit. Submitted late = 85%. Not submitted = Incomplete, which converts to zero at the end of the quarter if it is still missing.
Weekly Hours Log20%
Weekly Reflection — completion only, not scored for writing quality15%
Bootcamp Completion15%
Professional Growth — how well did you perform?50%
Scored on the 4-point scale below against the Illinois Essential Employability Skills.
Employer / Advisor Evaluation — Enterprise: Quarterly Review Board + client feedback20%
Employability Skills Self-Assessment — evidence cited from your own reflections15%
Capstone Presentation15%

The 4-point scale. A 3 does not mean "fine for a high schooler." It means you performed to the standard a paying employer expects.

4Excellent — exceeded the professional standard; took initiative beyond what was asked100%
3Meets — performed to the standard a paying employer expects92%
2Does Not Meet — inconsistent; would require correction from a supervisor75%
1Needs Serious Improvement — would jeopardize continued employment60%

"No Evidence" is not a score. If there is nothing to judge, the item is entered as Incomplete until evidence exists — you are never penalized for work that hasn't been observed yet, only for work you didn't submit.

How the two tracks are graded. Identical categories and weights. Placement-track students are evaluated by their site supervisor. Enterprise-track students are evaluated through a Quarterly Review Board — they present their work to a panel of adults and are scored on the same 10 employability skills — plus at least one client or advisor feedback form each quarter. Review Boards: Oct 15, Dec 16, Mar 11, with the fourth merging into the May capstone.
Special Course Policies
A fully signed Training Agreement (student, parent/guardian, employer, and school coordinator) must be on file before a student's first day of work — no exceptions. Performance or conduct violations are addressed per the Training Agreement and may result in removal from the program.
Documentation Gate — you must be current to work
Your weekly hours log and weekly reflection are due Sunday at 11:59 PM. If they are not submitted, you do not go to work that week until they are complete.
Placement track: not current, not on the schedule.
You report to class and finish your log and reflection instead of reporting to your placement site.
Enterprise track: not current, not remote.
Working off campus is a privilege earned by being current. If you are behind, you report to class on your A day until you are caught up. Bulldog Media students also lose equipment and studio access until current.
This is not a rule invented for school. Every contractor, freelancer, and hourly employee in the world gets paid by submitting their documentation on time. Repeated failure to stay current escalates to contact home, a written improvement plan, and ultimately a review of your Training Agreement.
Students with a transportation barrier, or an IEP or 504 accommodation that affects submission, should talk to the teacher — those are handled as accommodations before the gate is ever applied. A ride that falls through is an attendance problem to solve, not a compliance violation.

Learner Profile / BARK Chart
While students are graded on their academic achievement, students and parents also receive feedback on classroom and workplace behaviors and responsibility. While these behaviors are not directly graded, we believe constructive feedback should be given — both in the classroom and from your placement supervisor.
Extra Learning & Help Opportunities
Students can always come in before or after school for extra help with bootcamp deliverables, resume feedback, or interview practice. Communicate with your teacher to find a time that works for both of you.
Student Expectations for This Course
Treat your placement like a real job from Day 1 — punctuality, professional conduct, and follow-through matter as much there as any grade does here. Complete your weekly hours log and reflection on time every week. Communicate proactively with your teacher about scheduling conflicts, concerns, or issues at your placement — don't wait until it's a problem.
Characteristics of Successful Students in This Course
Reliable — shows up on time, every time. Communicates proactively rather than waiting to be asked. Takes initiative at their placement instead of waiting for direction. Treats feedback as a growth tool, not criticism. Takes ownership of their own professional appearance and conduct.
Teacher Expectations for This Course
I will help you find and succeed in a placement that fits your interests, and prepare you to walk in on Day 1 ready to perform like an employee, not just a visitor. I'll challenge you to take this seriously — because employers, your placement supervisor, and your future self will.
Remediation Statement
A missed weekly hours log or reflection is entered as Incomplete and can be submitted late for 85% credit at any point before the quarter closes. An Incomplete that is still missing when the quarter closes becomes a zero — so nothing is ever unrecoverable until the term ends. Incomplete bootcamp deliverables can be resubmitted for credit. Workplace performance concerns are addressed through direct communication with the employer or advisor and a written improvement plan, per the Training Agreement — not through grade remediation alone.
