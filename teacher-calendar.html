Both cohorts run the same 12 blocks in the same sequence — they just land on different calendar days. The Placement cohort (14 students, every day) finishes bootcamp Fri Aug 28 and starts at placement sites Mon Aug 31. The Enterprise cohort (10 students, A days only, full year) hits the same 12 blocks across A days and finishes Tue Sep 15. Two cohorts, one structure — you are never planning two different lessons.

What the two calendars show you at a glance
The Placement cohort's twelve blocks sit in three unbroken weeks. The Enterprise cohort's twelve are scattered across five, with a no-class day between almost every one — which is the real teaching problem in this course. Assume the A-day cohort forgets the thread between blocks. Open every Enterprise block with two minutes of "here's where we were," and don't schedule anything for them that depends on momentum from the previous session.

3Placement Cohort — Block by Block

Each block is one 90-minute period. Minute breakdowns are a plan, not a script — but the deliverable is not optional, because it's what the gradebook reads.

1
Thu Aug 13 · A Day · First Day of School
Begin With the End in Mind
Sets the tone
0–10
Welcome + the one question. "What do you want to be able to say about yourself in May that you can't say today?" Written, not discussed. Collect these — you'll hand them back at the capstone.
10–30
Show the capstone first. Walk the capstone requirements before anything else. Everything in this course is preparation for that presentation. Students who see the destination on day one make better choices in week six.
30–55
Syllabus + the gate. Cover grading honestly: half the grade is did you do the thing on time, half is how well did you do it. Then state the gate out loud — "Not current, not on the schedule." Say it on day one so it's never a surprise later.
55–75
Site walkthrough. Every student logs in live, in front of you. Find the weekly hours log, the reflection form, and your 10 skills pages. Do not let anyone leave without a confirmed login.
75–90
Career cluster preview + Training Agreement home. Quick overview of clusters to prime tomorrow's assessment. Training Agreement goes home tonight.
Prep & Materials
Syllabus copies · Training Agreements · projector for capstone walkthrough · student login list · index cards for the opening question
Deliverable
Confirmed site login + Activity 1 submitted. Training Agreement sent home.
2
Fri Aug 14 · B Day
Career & Self-Assessment
0–10
Training Agreement collection. Track who's missing — this is your first read on which students will need the escalation ladder.
10–45
Self-assessment. Students complete the interest and skills inventory on the site. Frame it as data for their own decision-making, not a test that assigns them a career.
45–70
Cluster deep dive. Students pick their top two clusters and research real local employers in each. This is quietly the groundwork for Block 5 — they should leave with names.
70–90
Pair share + baseline 10-skill self-rating. Each student rates themselves 4/3/2/1 on all 10 employability skills. Save this. It's the baseline the Q1 self-assessment gets measured against, and it's the single most persuasive artifact at the capstone.
Prep & Materials
Devices for all students · self-assessment.html · local employer list by cluster · baseline skill rating sheet
Deliverable
Self-assessment submitted · top 2 clusters named · baseline 10-skill rating on file
3
Mon Aug 17 · A Day
Your Professional Brand
0–15
The search. Students google themselves and write down exactly what a supervisor would find. Blunt, private, no shaming — the point is that this is already happening whether they manage it or not.
15–40
Email signature + phone communication. Every student builds their professional email signature — name, title (Field Experience Student), school, contact. Then the cold-call roleplay: phoning a potential employer. Most students have never done this and it shows.
40–70
The 7-item audit + LinkedIn. Work the social media audit checklist (4 items) and LinkedIn setup (3 items) — the submission is gated on all 7. Profile photo, public posts, bio, then LinkedIn photo, city, school, and a headline that says more than "student." Real changes made in class, not assigned as homework.
70–90
Brand statement. Two sentences: who you are professionally and what you're building toward. This becomes the opening line of the elevator pitch in Block 6, so make them write it down somewhere they'll find it.
Prep & Materials
Devices · phones allowed (cold-call roleplay) · real examples of digital footprints costing people jobs · LinkedIn walkthrough ready
Deliverable
All 7 checklist items complete (gates submission) · LinkedIn profile live · email signature submitted
4
Tue Aug 18 · B Day
Resume Workshop
0–15
What a resume is actually for. It's not a life summary — it's a 20-second argument that you're worth a conversation. Show one strong and one weak example side by side.
15–60
Build it. Work time on the template. Circulate constantly — most students need help translating fast food, babysitting, and sports into transferable-skill language. That translation is the lesson.
60–80
Peer review, structured. Trade with a partner. Three required questions: Is there a typo? Is every bullet a result rather than a duty? Would you call this person? Written feedback, not verbal.
80–90
Revise and submit. Resume must be submitted before students leave — tomorrow they apply with it. A resume that isn't done today blocks Block 5.
Prep & Materials
Resume template · strong/weak examples · peer review sheet · a printer if any placements want hard copies
Deliverable
Completed resume submitted — required before Block 5
5
Wed Aug 19 · A Day
Explore & Apply
Starts the paperwork clock
The most schedule-critical block in bootcamp
Applications submitted today start the employer paperwork clock. Every day this slips is a day a student's Aug 31 start date slips with it. If a student is absent, they make this up before Friday — not "sometime."
0–20
Walk the open positions. Go through the live positions list together. Name what each site actually wants and what the commute looks like — transportation reality kills more placements than interest does.
20–40
Rank three, honestly. Each student picks three placements in preference order and writes one sentence on why each fits their cluster work from Block 2. No one gets one option.
40–75
Apply. Application submitted with resume attached. Circulate and check every submission before it goes — a typo'd application to a partner site costs you the partner, not just the placement.
75–90
What happens next. Lay out the timeline: interviews, employer paperwork, confirmation in Block 11, start Aug 31. Set the expectation that you may need something from them on 24 hours' notice.
Prep & Materials
Positions list published and current · positions.html · resumes from Block 4 · transportation map
Deliverable
Application submitted with resume · 3 ranked preferences on file — no exceptions, no extensions
6
Thu Aug 20 · B Day
Elevator Pitch
0–15
Why 30 seconds. Model a strong pitch and a rambling one. The difference is structure, not charisma — which is good news for every nervous student in the room.
15–35
Draft. Start from the Block 3 brand statement. Structure: who I am → what I'm pursuing → what I bring → what I'm asking for.
35–70
Rehearse in rotation. Three different partners, 30 seconds each, one specific piece of feedback per round. Volume of reps beats depth of coaching here.
70–90
Record and submit. Phone video, 30 seconds, submitted. Recording is non-negotiable — students who hear themselves fix more than students you correct.
Prep & Materials
Pitch structure handout · timer · phones for recording · quiet corners or hallway space
Deliverable
30-second recorded pitch submitted
7
Fri Aug 21 · A Day
Interview Skills
0–20
STAR, taught properly. Situation, Task, Action, Result. Teach it hard today — STAR is also the format for every weekly reflection all semester, which means teaching it once here saves you fifteen weeks of vague submissions.
20–45
Question bank work. From interview-question-bank.html, students draft STAR answers to four questions, including "tell me about yourself" and one weakness question.
45–70
Partner practice with the bank. Partners draw questions at random. Answer cold. Being unprepared for the exact question is the actual skill.
70–90
Logistics for Monday. Confirm dress expectations, arrival time, partner names, and rotation order for the mock interview. Print the schedule and post it. Ambiguity Friday becomes chaos Monday.
Prep & Materials
STAR handout · interview-question-bank.html · Monday rotation schedule printed · confirmed partner list
Deliverable
4 written STAR answers submitted · student knows their Monday interview slot
8
Mon Aug 24 · B Day
Mock Interview
Community partners on site
−20–0
Partner arrival and briefing. Partners arrive 20 minutes early. Give each one the feedback form, the rotation schedule, and one sentence of direction: be warm but real — these students need honest signal, not encouragement.
0–10
Set the room. Students in professional dress, phones away, waiting area established. Ten minutes of ritual makes the next seventy feel real.
10–75
Rotation. 10-minute interviews, 3-minute resets. With 5–7 partners, all 14 students get through with room for overruns. Students not interviewing complete a structured observation form — watching is worth nearly as much as doing.
75–90
Partner debrief in front of students. Ask partners for the top two patterns they saw across the whole group. Hearing "most of you undersold yourselves" from a real employer lands differently than hearing it from a teacher.
Prep & Materials
Partners invited — confirm headcount by Aug 20 · mock-interview-feedback.html forms · rotation schedule · observation forms · water and coffee for partners · rooms or spaced tables
Deliverable
Completed interview + signed partner feedback form on file for every student
9
Tue Aug 25 · A Day
Improvement Plan + Meet Your 10 Employability Skills
0–30
Interview Improvement Plan. Students read their partner feedback and name two specific changes plus how they'll practice each. Feedback that isn't converted into a plan is just a compliment or a bruise.
30–60
The 10 skills, and how they're graded. Walk all 10 Illinois employability skills. Then show the 4/3/2/1 scale in plain language and be explicit about the standard: 3 means you performed to the level a paying employer expects — not "did fine for a high schooler."
60–80
How reflections feed the grade. Show the mechanism openly: each week's reflection is tagged to one skill, written in STAR, and at quarter close they self-score all 10 citing the week that proves it. Students who understand this write better reflections in week two.
80–90
Pick two focus skills. Each student names the two skills they'll target this semester and writes them into their goal sheet for Block 12.
Prep & Materials
Returned feedback forms · the 10 skill-*.html pages · 4/3/2/1 scale one-pager · goal sheet
Deliverable
Improvement Plan submitted · 2 focus skills named
10
Wed Aug 26 · B Day
Workplace Dilemma Stations
0–10
Setup and rules. 15 stations across 7 clusters, printed from dilemma-scenarios.html. Pairs, ~5 minutes per station, written response required at each. No right answers — defensible reasoning only.
10–75
Rotate. Circulate and listen more than you intervene. Note which dilemmas produce real disagreement — those are your debrief material.
75–90
Debrief the hard three. Take the three most contested dilemmas whole-group. Land the point that ties them together: almost every workplace problem is solved by telling someone early, not by handling it alone.
Prep & Materials
15 station cards printed and placed before class · response sheets · timer · room set for rotation
Deliverable
Completed station response sheet (all 15)
11
Thu Aug 27 · A Day
Post-Assessment + Placement Confirmed
0–25
Post-assessment. Same instrument as Block 2's baseline. Students compare then and now themselves — two weeks of growth is visible and worth naming out loud.
25–50
Placement confirmations. Hand out site, supervisor name, address, start time, dress code, and reporting instructions. Every student writes their own start details down — don't let the only copy be a paper you handed them.
50–75
Goal conferences begin. Start 1-on-1s while the rest of the class works on Block 12 materials. Five minutes each: two focus skills, one measurable semester goal, one thing they're nervous about. Finish the rest tomorrow.
75–90
Logistics gauntlet. Out loud, student by student: how are you getting there, what time do you leave, what's your supervisor's name, what do you do if you're sick? Transportation problems surface here or they surface on Aug 31 as a no-show.
Prep & Materials
Post-assessment · signed employer paperwork complete · printed placement detail sheets · goal conference form
Deliverable
Post-assessment submitted · placement details confirmed in writing · transportation plan verified

Fri Aug 28 · B Day
Internship Plan of Success + Launch Day
Bootcamp completion scored
0–35
Internship Plan of Success. Written and submitted: two focus skills, semester goal, weekly hours target, what success looks like at this specific site. This is the document you'll hand back at the capstone.
35–55
Finish goal conferences. Anyone remaining from yesterday.
55–75
The weekly rhythm, walked live. Log hours as you go, reflection due Sunday 11:59 PM. Everyone submits a practice reflection right now, in class, so no one's first attempt is unsupervised. Then restate the gate: "Not current, not on the schedule."
75–90
Launch. Hand back the day-one index cards. First shift is Monday. Send them out with the reminder that from Monday they represent the school, the program, and the next student who wants that placement.

Plan of Success submitted · goal conference complete · practice reflection submitted — all three required to score Bootcamp Completion (15%)

Be reachable all day. Expect at least one transportation failure, one student who can't find the entrance, and one supervisor who wasn't told a student was coming. Have every supervisor's cell number in your phone before Monday morning, not in a binder.

4Enterprise Cohort — Block by Block

Nine of these twelve blocks are the Placement lesson, unchanged. Only Blocks 5, 8, and 11 are rebuilt, and the framing shifts throughout: their supervisor is a client, and their business is the placement.

Run these as one lesson, not two
On the shared blocks, teach the same content and adjust the examples in the moment. Where the Placement cohort hears "your supervisor," Enterprise hears "your client." Where Placement hears "your site," Enterprise hears "your business." Don't build a parallel curriculum — you'll abandon it by October.
1
Thu Aug 13 · A Day · First Day of School
Begin With the End in Mind
Same block as Placement
0–10
Same opening question, written and collected.
10–30
Show the May capstone. Enterprise students have a full year, which cuts both ways — more room to build something real, and far more room to drift. Naming May on day one is how you fight the drift.
30–55
Syllabus + the Enterprise gate. Same grading model. Different gate: "Not current, not remote." Working off-campus is a privilege earned by being current. State plainly that Bulldog Media students also lose equipment and studio access.
55–75
Site walkthrough. Confirmed login before anyone leaves. Emphasize the hours log — self-directed students under-log, and unlogged hours are ungradeable hours.
75–90
Preview the Quarterly Review Board. Boards are Oct 15 · Dec 16 · Mar 11, with Q4 merging into the May capstone. Say now that they present to adults who aren't you. It changes how they work in September.

Tue Aug 25 · A Day
Define Your Enterprise
Replaces Explore & Apply
The block that makes the year gradeable
Placement students get their scope defined by an employer. Enterprise students have to define their own — and a student whose enterprise is still vague in November is a student you cannot grade, coach, or defend at a review board. Do not let anyone leave today without a written scope.
0–20
Name the enterprise. What is the business, who is the customer, what do they pay for? One paragraph, no jargon. If they can't say who pays, they don't have a business yet — they have an interest, and today's job is to narrow it.
20–45
Scope of work. What will exist by December that doesn't exist today? By May? Concrete deliverables, not aspirations. Bulldog Media students scope against the actual production calendar — games, events, announcements — so their year has real deadlines in it.
45–70
Hours plan. 60 hours is the floor, and most of this cohort will pass it easily. The real work is deciding when: which days, how long, logged how. Self-directed students fail on logging, not effort.
70–90
Name your client or advisor. Every student identifies at least one external person who will complete a feedback form — a paying client, a recurring customer, or the Bulldog Media advisor. No name today means no external voice in their grade, so chase this one.
Prep & Materials
Enterprise scope template · client feedback form sample · Bulldog Media production calendar · hours planning sheet
Deliverable
Written enterprise scope (Dec + May deliverables) · hours plan · named client or advisor — required before Block 8

5When It Doesn't Go To Plan

Twelve blocks with no slack means absences and interruptions need a rule, not a decision each time.

Absence rules by block
  • Blocks 4, 5, 8 — must be made up, on a deadline. Resume, application, and mock interview/pitch each gate something downstream. Resume and application: made up within 48 hours. A missed mock interview or pitch panel: scheduled with you 1-on-1 within a week, because there's no second panel.
  • Blocks 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 10 — asynchronous makeup. Every one has a submittable deliverable on the site. Assign it and move on; don't reteach.
  • Blocks 11, 12 — cannot be skipped. Confirmation, goal conference, and Plan of Success are the three things that let a student start work at all. A student who misses these doesn't launch on time, and that's the honest consequence, not a punishment.
If you lose a block to an assembly, fire drill, or schedule change
Merge, don't cut. The natural pairs are Blocks 2+3 (both largely independent work time) and Blocks 9+10 (the improvement plan compresses to 20 minutes if you've already returned the feedback). Never merge Blocks 4, 5, or 8 into anything — and never cut Block 1, since the tone it sets is the reason the gate works in October.
If a placement falls through mid-bootcamp
It happens most years. The student keeps moving through the blocks with the cohort — the bootcamp content is identical regardless of site — while you work the placement problem separately. Do not park a student in a corner "until we figure it out"; they'll disengage in three days and you'll spend the semester recovering it.
