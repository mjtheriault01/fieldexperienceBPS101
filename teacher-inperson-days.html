In-Person Day Plan

Field Experience · Placement cohort · every-other-Tuesday class days

Placement Once placements begin, Placement students are at their site most days — but return to school every other Tuesday for a 90-minute in-person class. This is the plan for that day: three fixed parts, same structure every time, so students and you both know what to expect without re-explaining it each session. The Enterprise cohort (A days) is unaffected — it already meets on its own calendar; see Teacher Calendar.

The seven in-person Tuesdays
Sep 15 · Sep 29 · Oct 13 · Oct 27 · Nov 12 · Nov 24 · Dec 8 — the last one before capstone (tentatively Dec 15). Not a perfectly even two-week cadence throughout (Nov 12 breaks the pattern), so treat this list, not a recurring rule, as the source of truth if building a calendar invite or slide deck.

The Structure — Three Parts, Every Time

A 90-minute block splits roughly 25 / 35 / 25, with 5 minutes of slack for transitions. Adjust the split by a few minutes per session as needed — the order and purpose of each part should not change.

Part 1 · ~25 min
Discussion — Speed-Dating Reflection
Rotate every 3 min

Two rows or two concentric circles of chairs, facing each other. You call out a question; each pair talks for ~3 minutes; you call "rotate" and one row/circle shifts one seat. New question, new partner. The questions are pulled straight from the weekly reflection form students already fill out at their placement — this is the same reflecting they're already doing, just spoken instead of typed, and to a peer instead of a form.

Room setup for ~14 students

Two rows of 7 chairs facing each other (or two concentric circles, inner facing out). Each round, the outer row/circle moves one seat to the right; the inner row stays put. Odd numbers: one group of three, or you fill the gap and rotate through yourself.

Question Deck

Pick 5–6 per session, not all 8 — that's roughly the 25-minute budget at 3 min/round. Vary which ones you use across the seven sessions so it doesn't feel scripted, and don't hesitate to swap in a question that comes up naturally from something a student says.

Wildcard — add your own livee.g. "what's the most 'adult' thing you've had to do at your placement so far?" React to what's actually happening in the room that week.
One question is deliberately left out
The reflection form's ninth question — "something my teacher should know / concerns or support you may need" — is written for a private audience of one. Don't put it in the peer rotation. If it comes up in casual conversation between partners, fine, but it belongs in a 1:1 with you (Part 2/3 circulation), not spoken to a rotating stranger.
Part 2 · ~35 min
Capstone — Teach + Work Time
5 min teach · 30 min build

Open with a short segment on that week's capstone focus (see the calendar below), then let students build. This is the recurring, ongoing block where the Capstone actually gets made — not a single cram session in December. Reference tools: Capstone Details · Capstone Rubric · Portfolio Guide · Pitch Prep Sheet.

The rubric is intentionally broad
capstone-rubric.html already does exactly what was asked here — it doesn't prescribe what the portfolio has to contain (a welding student and an accounting student shouldn't bring the same things), but it does score everyone on the same five criteria: evidence, range, curation, ownership, professionalism. That's the model to keep repeating out loud during these work-time openings: "any format, these five things must be true of it."

Where portfolio artifacts actually live
Students keep a "Field Experience" folder in their own Google Drive and save artifacts there as they go — not through portfolio.html on the student portal (which writes to Supabase and sits in the same SOPPA-affected bucket as the hours/reflection tools, see the Google-native rebuild notes). This is deliberate: it's already fully Google-native, works throughout the semester without waiting on any tooling decision, and each student owns their own folder. Say this explicitly in Session 1's capstone mini-teach — "start your Field Experience folder today" — so nobody is scrambling to assemble a portfolio from scratch in December.
Part 3 · ~25 min
Employability Skills — Packet Work Time
2 skills / session

Students work through two skill worksheets from the Employability Skills Packet, solo or with a partner, without teacher instruction — each worksheet already carries the scenario, the concept, and the activities on the page. This frees you to do what actually needs a teacher: circulate and hold 1:1 conversations about placement experiences, businesses, or whatever came up in Part 1.

Answer key exists — keep it off the student tables
employability-answer-key.html has the correct Activity 1 response and one-line reasoning for all 10 skills, for your own reference while circulating. It's a separate page from the student packet on purpose — there's no risk of it accidentally going out with a stack of worksheets.
Open item — how this gets graded
The packet isn't yet mapped to a category in the grading model (Bootcamp / Weekly Tasks / Capstone / Employer Feedback, each 25% — see Grading Policy). Options: fold it into Weekly Tasks as a light completion check, treat it as ungraded formative practice (the self-rating circles are the point, not a score), or create a small new line. Left open deliberately — flag before quarter grades are calculated, same as the Skill Indicators activity's open grading question.
