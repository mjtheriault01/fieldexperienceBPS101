Field Experience · all 16 activities · built from bootcamp.html

Every task in the bootcamp, in order, exactly as students see it — with a co-teach code on each activity and blank space to write your own pacing. Nothing here is locked. The point of printing it is to argue with it.

How to use this

Read down the list and decide two things per activity: which day it lands on and whether you teach it together or apart. The co-teach codes are a recommendation based on how much of each activity is genuinely identical for both cohorts — not a constraint. Write your day assignments in the margin, then transfer them to the pacing worksheet at the back.

The short answer on co-teaching
11 of the 16 activities are fully co-teachable, 3 need a partial split, and only 2 are genuinely separate lessons. The two that cannot be shared are the two where the Placement cohort points at an outside employer and the Enterprise cohort has none: Activity 5 (Explore & Apply) and Activity 13 (Placement Confirmed). Everything else is either identical or the same lesson with different examples.
  • Activity 10 (Dilemma Stations) and Activity 14 (Goal Conference) are better together, not just cheaper. 15 stations support more pairs than one cohort fills, and the debrief gets sharper when placement and enterprise students argue from different positions. The goal conferences are 1-on-1s — two teachers working in parallel cuts 168 minutes down to 84.
  • Activity 8 (Mock Interview) is the one to plan carefully. Step 1, peer speed interviews, is better with 24 students. Step 2 needs outside adults, and combining cohorts means 24 students needing interviewers on the same day instead of 14.
Read this before you assign days — the pacing decision only you can make
Content alignment and calendar alignment are two different things. The Placement cohort meets every day and the Enterprise cohort meets A days only, so Placement covers roughly two activities for every one the Enterprise cohort covers. Under the dated calendar as it currently stands, only Day 1 (Thu Aug 13) has both cohorts doing the same activity. After that they drift apart immediately — by Aug 19 Placement is on Activity 5 while Enterprise is on Activity 3.
  • To actually co-teach, the shared lessons have to be pinned to A days, and the Placement-only work has to fill the B days. That caps you at roughly six shared blocks in the Aug 13–28 window.
  • The cost of doing that: Explore & Apply gets pushed to a B day after the resume workshop, which moves applications from Aug 19 to Aug 24 or later. That is five fewer days on the employer paperwork clock before the Aug 31 start.
  • The cost of not doing it: you teach all 12 blocks twice, and the second cohort's version is the one that gets thin when the week gets busy.
This is a real trade between co-teaching efficiency and placement lead time, and it depends on how fast your employers actually turn paperwork around. That is your call, not the document's — the worksheet at the back is there for whichever way you go.

Explore & Apply
Browse job menu · Research employers · Meet with teacher · Apply · Follow-up
Separate ~90 min · Day 4–5 (apply over weekend)
What we do in class
  • Browse the placement list on this site — explore what's available across all 7 career clusters
  • Employer scavenger hunt: research your top 2–3 choices. Find out what they do, who works there, what they value
  • Match your skills from your resume to the placements you're interested in
  • Meet with your teacher to talk through your choices and confirm your application plan
  • Submit your application(s) — this starts the clock on employer paperwork, so the sooner the better
Employer research — know before you go
  • Before applying: look up their website, LinkedIn, and Google reviews. What do they do? Who works there? Did they recently expand or hire?
  • Goal: walk into your interview able to say "I noticed you just opened a second location" or "I saw your team focuses heavily on youth programs" — not "so what do you guys do again?"
After you apply
  • Send a follow-up email 5–7 business days after applying if you haven't heard back — brief, professional, confirms your interest
  • Send a thank-you email within 24 hours of any interview — one paragraph, reference something specific from the conversation, confirm you want the role
  • Both emails go in your portfolio — they're evidence of professional communication, not just formalities

8
Mock Interview
Peer speed interviews · Formal mock · Real feedback · Question bank
Split part ~90 min
Step 1 — Peer speed interviews (whole class)
  • Class splits in half: one side interviews, the other answers. Each pair gets 2–3 questions from the interview question bank, then rotate down the line.
  • After 30 minutes, switch sides — the interviewers become interviewees. Everyone practices both roles.
  • Interviewees don't know which questions are coming. That's the point — you get comfortable answering live, unrehearsed, for a rotating set of people.
Step 2 — Formal mock interview (community partner)
  • 1-on-1 with a business partner, chamber member, or administrator — ideally someone you don't know well
  • Your interviewer uses a structured feedback form. You receive written feedback immediately after — specific, honest, actionable.
  • Goal is two interviews each during this block. When you're not in the chair, review the feedback from your last one.
  • No one gets a perfect mock interview. That's the point. You find out what to fix before the real one.

10
Workplace Dilemma Stations
15 dilemmas · Carousel stations · Career clusters · Class debrief
Co-teach ~90 min
What we do in class
  • Carousel activity: 15 dilemma stations set up around the room. Small groups rotate every few minutes, read the scenario, and discuss what they'd do.
  • Scenarios span all 7 career clusters — a healthcare confidentiality situation, a manufacturing safety shortcut, a supervisor asking something that feels wrong in an education setting, a customer conflict in retail, and more
  • After rotating, the class picks 2–3 scenarios to debrief together — groups share what they decided and why
  • You'll disagree with each other. That's the activity. There's often no single right answer — what matters is how you think it through.
  • If you were absent: all 15 dilemma scenarios are linked here so you can review them and mark complete

Goal Conference
Prep your goals · One-on-one with your teacher · Get expectations cleared
Co-teach 5–10 min per student · Runs across 1–2 class days
Come prepared
  • Before your conference, draft your 2–3 goals. Your teacher shouldn't be the one prompting you to come up with them — arrive with answers ready.
  • Think through: What do you want to get out of this placement? What skill do you most want to improve? What would make this semester feel worth it?

What we do in class
  • 5–10 minute one-on-one with your teacher — not a check-in, a real conversation about your placement and what you want from it
  • Set 2–3 goals for your semester: specific, achievable, and tied to what you actually care about
  • Review the time log and reflection process one more time — everyone does a test submission so there are no surprises
While you wait for your conference
  • Conferences run 1–2 full class days. When you're not in the chair, you'll get started on the first in-depth employability skill lesson or continue refining your elevator pitch for the capstone.
